Cue Exposure Therapy + tDCS, and Non-VR Technology Platforms for CET in Substance Use Disorders: A Research Review (May 2026)
TL;DR

tDCS + cue exposure: Anodal tDCS over the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) reliably reduces acute cue-induced craving for nicotine, methamphetamine and cocaine, but the evidence that pairing tDCS with cue exposure or cognitive bias modification (CBM) enhances extinction or reduces relapse is mixed-to-null — three of the four published combined tDCS+CBM RCTs in alcohol users reported null primary outcomes, and pooled meta-analyses still rate the effect as "medium but highly variable, less robust than rTMS" (Mehta et al., Neuropsychopharmacology 2024).
Non-VR CET platforms exist and are growing, with the largest evidence base for smartphone-delivered Cognitive Bias Modification (notably the Australian SWiPE/AAT-App and the Dutch Breindebaas app) and for the Danish Mellentin smartphone CET app — but the most recent, largest RCTs of both approaches (Mellentin 2019 alcohol CET app; Garfield 2026 AAT-App RCT) were null on their primary endpoints, while a 2025 systematic review (Thaysen-Petersen et al.) found that technology-assisted CET reduced craving (60% vs 17% of NT-CET studies) and consumption (80% vs 38%) more often than non-technology CET, almost entirely driven by VR studies (14 of 23 T-CET trials were VR; only 9 used non-VR technology).
Bottom line for clinical use (May 2026): Neither tDCS-augmented CET nor non-VR digital CET platforms are yet "evidence-based" stand-alone treatments. The strongest empirical support is for (a) right-anodal DLPFC tDCS as an adjunct for cue-induced craving in stimulant and tobacco use disorders, and (b) approach-bias modification (ApBM) when delivered to inpatient/residential AUD samples; outpatient app delivery and tDCS+CBM combinations have so far failed to replicate these effects.

Key Findings
Topic 1 — tDCS combined with cue exposure / CBM

The mechanistic rationale is well established. The DLPFC is the canonical target because it exerts top-down control over the salience/reward circuitry driving cue-induced craving. Most studies use 2 mA anodal stimulation for 20 minutes with the anode over F4 (right DLPFC) and the cathode over F3 or contralateral supraorbital, in 1–10 sessions.
Acute online effects on cue-induced craving are robust for nicotine and stimulants. Fregni et al. (2008) — the seminal cross-over trial in 24 smokers — showed both left and right DLPFC anodal tDCS significantly reduced cue-provoked craving versus sham (≈20% craving decrease, p = .007). Mondino et al. (2018; n = 29 TUD) found 10 sessions of right-DLPFC tDCS significantly reduced craving and altered posterior-cingulate cue reactivity on fMRI. Kang et al. (2019, Addictive Behaviors; doi:10.1016/j.addbeh.2019.05.006), a meta-analysis of 12 nicotine tDCS trials, reported a moderate pooled effect on cue-provoked craving (effect size = 0.422, p = .004) and on smoking intake (effect size = 0.557, p = .004), with the right-anodal DLPFC montage most efficacious.
Methamphetamine/cocaine. Shahbabaie et al. (2014; n = 32) reported a state-dependent effect — anodal right DLPFC tDCS reduced craving at rest but increased craving during cue exposure. Batista, Klauss et al. (2015; n = 17 crack-cocaine) found bilateral DLPFC tDCS reduced craving. Alizadehgoradel et al. (2020, Brain Stimul 13:582-593; n = 60 MUD) — a methodologically strong double-blind parallel RCT — found 10 sessions of bilateral DLPFC tDCS improved executive function and reduced craving versus sham. However, Ekhtiari et al. (2022; n = 60 MUD; Hum Brain Mapp, doi:10.1002/hbm.26007) — the most rigorous triple-blind sham-controlled tDCS+fMRI cue-reactivity study — found no active-vs-sham difference in self-reported craving and observed that the global decrease in brain cue reactivity occurred only in the sham group, raising the possibility that a single tDCS session may block normal within-session habituation.
Alcohol use disorder. Klauss et al. (2014; n = 33 outpatients): 5 sessions of bilateral DLPFC tDCS halved relapse rates (8/16 vs 15/17) at 6 months but did not change craving. Klauss et al. (2018, Front Pharmacol 9:716; doi:10.3389/fphar.2018.00716; n = 35 inpatients): a longer protocol with more sessions reduced craving and 3-month relapse. Holla et al. (2020) and Gairola et al. (2024, Indian J Psychiatry) reported transient craving reductions that faded by 8 weeks.
tDCS + CBM combination trials — three of four null, one positive. This is the most directly relevant body of evidence for the user's question:

den Uyl, Gladwin & Wiers (2016, Alcohol Clin Exp Res 40:2124-2133; doi:10.1111/acer.13171): hazardous drinkers; tDCS + alcohol approach-bias retraining; null on the primary behavioural outcome but modulated ERPs, with a non-significant trend toward reduced 1-year relapse in the unilateral-anodal DLPFC + CBM group.
den Uyl, Gladwin & Wiers (2018, Alcohol Clin Exp Res; doi:10.1111/acer.13841; n = 83 alcohol-dependent inpatients): tDCS + Attentional Bias Modification — null on craving and drinking; active tDCS over DLPFC showed a non-significant directional benefit on relapse.
Claus et al. (2019, Alcohol Clin Exp Res; doi:10.1111/acer.14091; n = 79 at-risk drinkers): 2 mA anodal tDCS over the right inferior frontal cortex + CBM; null effects on both approach bias and drinking.
Wang et al. (CCAT + tDCS in 73 female methamphetamine users): 20 sessions of bilateral DLPFC tDCS + computerized cognitive addiction therapy (CCAT) significantly reduced craving vs sham CCAT and vs control — by far the most positive combined trial, and the most intensive (20 sessions).


Pooled meta-analytic estimates.

Chan, Chang, Lu & Goh (2024, Psychiatry Res 331:115621; doi:10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115621): 43 sham-controlled trials, n = 2,008. tDCS produced a moderate reduction in craving overall, with effects driven by bilateral montages, right anodal DLPFC, 1.5–2 mA, ≥20 min, and ≥35 cm² electrodes. tDCS reduced cravings for opioids, methamphetamine, cocaine and tobacco but not for alcohol or cannabis.
Mehta et al. (2024, Neuropsychopharmacology 49:649-680; doi:10.1038/s41386-023-01776-0) — 36 tDCS studies, n = 1,582 — confirmed that tDCS effects are "medium but highly variable and less robust than rTMS." AUD meta-analyses were null for both single-session craving (SMD = −0.60, 95% CI: −1.22 to 0.01, p = .06; 4 trials) and multi-session craving (SMD = −0.14, 95% CI: −0.57 to 0.28, p = .51; 8 trials) and for AUD consumption (SMD = −0.08, p = .62). The review concluded that "tDCS studies have shown similar efficacy to rTMS in the treatment of tobacco, stimulant, and opioid use disorders... while alcohol studies have shown less consistent results." Right-anodal DLPFC was most efficacious.
Chen et al. (2020, Front Psychiatry 11:598): 32 studies on substance/food craving — significant effect of tDCS overall; modulators include current intensity, montage and number of sessions.


Key research groups: Ekhtiari & Shahbabaie (Iran/Laureate Institute, Tulsa) on methamphetamine; Klauss & Nakamura-Palacios (Brazil/Federal University of Espírito Santo) on AUD and crack-cocaine; Nitsche (Bochum) on stimulation parameters; Fregni (Harvard/Spaulding); Mondino & Brunelin (Lyon) on smoking; Wiers, den Uyl & Gladwin (University of Amsterdam ADAPT lab) on tDCS+CBM; Salehinejad & Alizadehgoradel (Iran/Bochum) on DLPFC+executive function; George & Mehta (CAMH/Toronto) on synthesis.
Ongoing/registered trials (ClinicalTrials.gov): NCT03382379 (Ekhtiari MUD tDCS-fMRI); NCT04135599 (bilateral DLPFC tDCS in alcoholics); NCT04990375 (tDCS to prevent relapse in AUD); NCT05426460 (AAT + tDCS for smoking cue reactivity — the most directly relevant CET+tDCS RCT currently in progress, combining personalised multi-cue exposure with an Approach/Avoidance Task and DLPFC tDCS).

Topic 2 — Non-VR digital platforms for CET

Mellentin smartphone CET app (Denmark) — the only purpose-built non-VR app explicitly delivering classical cue-exposure-with-urge-specific-coping-skills (CET-USCS). Design described in Mellentin et al. (2017, JMIR mHealth uHealth 5(1):e5; doi:10.2196/mhealth.6500). The investigator-blinded RCT (Mellentin et al. 2019, JMIR mHealth uHealth 7(8):e13793; doi:10.2196/13793; n = 164 AUD outpatients, 93% follow-up, NCT02298751) compared CET-USCS app vs group CET-USCS vs CBT alone. Result: null primary outcome — neither CET-USCS arm improved drinking outcomes beyond CBT at 6-month follow-up; the app was, however, non-inferior to in-person group CET.
Computer/video-based CET (pre-VR generation; from Thaysen-Petersen 2025 review):

Nattala et al. (2018, India) — video-enabled cue-exposure-based intervention improved post-discharge drinking outcomes in AUD inpatients.
Pollak et al. (2021) — personalised smoking cue exposure using up to nine personal smoking-related images on a computer.
O'Brien et al. (1990; cocaine) — videotape of a person administering cocaine, the canonical computer-presented cue paradigm.
Du et al. (2014, OUD) — videos of a man smoking or injecting heroin.
de Quirós Aragón (2005, OUD) — videos depicting opioid seeking, use, withdrawal, overdose.
Geisel et al. (2016) — monitor-based picture cue exposure for AUD.
Hernández-Serrano et al. (2020), Weber et al. (2023), Ng et al. (2024, Alcohol 115:53-59) — computer- or projector-delivered complex-cue exposure for AUD.


Smartphone-delivered cognitive bias modification (CBM):

Breindebaas app (Netherlands; Laurens et al. 2020, JMIR mHealth uHealth; doi:10.2196/16217; n = 1,082) — open-label feasibility of alcohol avoidance training (push/pull picture task) showed reductions in weekly alcohol use; not yet RCT-confirmed.
SWiPE/AAT-App (Australia; Manning, Garfield et al., Monash/Turning Point) — gamified, personalised approach-bias modification. Open-label feasibility (Manning et al. 2021, JMIR mHealth uHealth 9(12):e31353; doi:10.2196/31353) was positive on craving and consumption. The definitive double-blind RCT (Garfield et al. 2026, Addiction; doi:10.1111/add.70184; n = 79 outpatient AUD) reported a null primary outcome: week-4 standard drinks did not differ between ApBM and sham (75.5 vs 71.3 drinks; difference 4.16, 95% CI −42.4 to 50.7, p = .859). Only one delayed secondary effect at week 16 reached significance (β = −12.5, p = .030). Craving (CEQ-F) was non-significant.
Adaptive A-ApBM (China; pilot RCT n = 136 methamphetamine users; JMIR 2024) — significant cue-induced craving reduction at post-intervention (Cohen d = 0.34, p < .01) and 16-week follow-up (d = 0.40, p = .01) for adaptive ApBM but not for static ApBM or no-intervention controls.
MAAT trial (Garfield et al. 2021 protocol, Trials 22:21; doi:10.1186/s13063-020-04927-6) — multi-site RCT of personalised ApBM smartphone delivery for methamphetamine use disorder; results pending.


Attentional bias modification (ABM) — dot-probe based platforms. Heitmann et al. (2018, Syst Rev 7:160; doi:10.1186/s13643-018-0822-6) systematically reviewed 18 ABM trials (9 alcohol, 6 nicotine, 3 opiate): 10/18 found symptom changes, and 8/10 multi-session ABM trials showed positive effects. Specific platforms include the Alcohol Attention Control Training Programme (Fadardi & Cox) and the standard dot-probe with alcohol-vs-neutral stimuli. The 2016 Cristea et al. meta-analysis on CBM was less positive (small to null pooled effects).
Ecological momentary intervention (EMI) apps that use cue-based logic:

Craving-Manager app (France; Auriacombe group, Frontiers Psychiatry 2023; doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1143167) — multicenter RCT in progress; uses EMA + just-in-time-adaptive intervention triggered by geolocation when participants approach personally registered risky cue locations.
Nałogometr app (Poland; Obarska et al. 2022, Front Psychiatry 13:1011585; NCT05434429) — EMA-based CBT/mindfulness intervention for craving and lapse risk; results forthcoming.
Tetris EMI for alcohol craving in women (Hahn CK et al., 2026, Drug Alcohol Depend 279:113052; n = 40 women) — uses visuospatial interference; "Tetris did not reduce daily craving or drinking" (null pilot result).


Reframe app (US, commercial) — markets itself as combining CET principles with daily neuroscience-based exercises; no peer-reviewed RCT; the cited tDCS/cue-exposure science is borrowed from the literature above.
The most rigorous synthesis to date — Thaysen-Petersen et al. (2025, Front Psychiatry 16:1544763; doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1544763): systematic review of 44 controlled trials (35 RCTs). NT-CET n = 21 (AUD = 13, NUD = 5, OUD = 2, mixed = 1); T-CET n = 23, of which 14 were VR and only 9 used non-VR technology (computers/projectors/monitors/smartphones). Headline findings: technology-assisted CET reported significant reduction in craving (60%) and consumption (80%) more often than NT-CET (17% craving; 38% consumption) — but the authors caution this advantage is "particularly when delivered in virtual reality" (88% of VR studies favored CET on consumption). No study reported effect sizes on craving, and only one study reported a small effect of NT-CET on consumption at 6/12 months (Monti 2001). The single direct head-to-head non-VR T-CET vs NT-CET comparison (Mellentin 2019) found no difference up to 6 months. Risk of bias was high in 25/44 trials, with concerns in 17/44 and low in only 2/44.

Cross-cutting observations

The single best methodological comparison of NT-CET vs non-VR T-CET (Mellentin 2019) was null, so we cannot yet claim non-VR digital CET is superior to in-clinic CET.
The single best methodological comparison of tDCS + CBM vs sham (Claus 2019) was null. Wang's positive CCAT + tDCS trial used 20 sessions in inpatients — far more intensive than other negative trials — suggesting dose-response may matter.
VR-CET (out of scope but the relevant comparator) consistently outperforms non-VR T-CET on craving induction realism (Hernández-Serrano, Ghiţă; ALCO-VR software) and has begun to show RCT-level efficacy. Huang Q, Chen X, Tang Y, Wang X et al. (2025, Transl Psychiatry 15:318; doi:10.1038/s41398-025-03553-7; n = 89 men with methamphetamine use disorder, 16 sessions over 8 weeks) found both CET and CET-augmented (CETA) VR arms produced significant reductions in tonic craving post-intervention (CET p = .001; CETA p = .010), while the no-stimulation group showed no change (p = .217).

Recommendations
Stage 1 — Pilot / proof-of-concept (next 6 months): If launching a new tDCS+CET program, adopt the parameters with the most consistent positive signal: anode F4 (right DLPFC), cathode F3 or contralateral supraorbital, 2 mA, 20 min, ≥10 sessions, electrodes ≥35 cm². Combine with personalised, multi-trial cue exposure (image or video). Target stimulant or nicotine populations first, where pooled tDCS effects are positive (Chan 2024, Mehta 2024); avoid AUD as the lead indication given consistent null meta-analytic findings (Mehta 2024 AUD multi-session craving SMD = −0.14, p = .51).
Stage 2 — Confirmatory RCT design: Require ≥150 participants, double-blind, sham-controlled, with cue-induced craving (VAS) as primary acute outcome and 3- and 6-month relapse as primary clinical outcome. Pre-specify habituation slope (pre-vs-post within-session) as a mechanistic outcome to detect the Ekhtiari 2022 "blocked habituation" pattern. Include a tDCS + CBM/CET arm separate from tDCS alone, with at least 10 stimulation sessions (mirroring Wang's positive CCAT design rather than the brief 4-session den Uyl protocols).
Stage 3 — Digital platform choice for non-VR CET: For alcohol craving in community/outpatient settings, the AAT-App/SWiPE platform is mature, free, and personalisable but failed its primary outcome in 2026 — adopt only as adjunct, not as monotherapy. For inpatient/residential settings, ApBM has stronger meta-analytic support (Eberl, Wiers, Manning et al.) and should be the default digital adjunct. For aftercare delivery of cue exposure, the Mellentin app is the only validated platform but did not improve on group CET. Avoid commercial apps without peer-reviewed RCTs (e.g. Reframe).
Decision benchmarks that would change these recommendations:

A confirmatory tDCS+CET RCT (e.g., the in-progress NCT05426460 AAT + tDCS smoking trial) showing ≥30% reduction in 6-month relapse vs sham (Cohen d ≥ 0.4 sustained) → tDCS+CET becomes first-line adjunct.
The MAAT trial reporting a significant reduction in methamphetamine use → adopt personalised smartphone ApBM as standard adjunct in stimulant treatment.
Independent replication of Wang's CCAT+tDCS finding in a registered, double-blind methamphetamine trial → adopt computerised cognitive training + tDCS as a combined modality.

Caveats

High heterogeneity: I² values in the Mehta 2024 and Chan 2024 meta-analyses are typically 70–95%, indicating substantial between-study variability driven by sample, stimulation parameters and cue paradigms.
Single sessions vs multi-session: Acute craving effects do not reliably translate to clinical outcomes — many trials show post-stimulation craving reductions that fade within weeks (Gairola 2024 explicitly showed this).
State-dependent effects: Shahbabaie 2014 and Ekhtiari 2022 raise the unresolved possibility that tDCS during active cue exposure may increase short-term craving or block habituation; this is the opposite of the intended extinction-learning mechanism and must be empirically resolved before scaling tDCS+CET protocols.
Publication bias: Open-label and feasibility studies of digital CBM/CET apps are uniformly positive, whereas the blinded RCTs (Mellentin 2019, Garfield 2026, Claus 2019, den Uyl 2018, Hahn 2026 Tetris pilot) are uniformly null or trend-level — a hallmark pattern of efficacy attenuation under rigorous control.
Most "non-VR CET platform" studies actually deliver CBM, not classical cue exposure with response prevention. This conceptual conflation is widespread in the literature and inflates apparent evidence for "digital CET."
Commercial claims often outrun evidence. Marketing materials (e.g., Reframe's claim that tDCS + cue exposure "reduces alcohol cravings by up to 50% when combined with behavioral therapies") overstate the heterogeneous and partly null meta-analytic record; the underlying primary trials (Klauss 2014, 2018) showed reduced relapse but inconsistent craving effects, and Mehta 2024 found no significant pooled effect on AUD craving or consumption.
Sample sizes remain small. Most tDCS+CET/CBM trials have n < 100; effect-size estimates are unstable.
No published RCT directly combines tDCS with the Mellentin smartphone CET app, the SWiPE app, or VR-CET in alcohol use disorder — the most clinically logical next combination remains untested as of May 2026. The closest in-progress trial (NCT05426460, AAT + tDCS for smoking) is in nicotine, not alcohol.

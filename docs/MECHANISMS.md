# Behavioral mechanisms & evidence

This document explains *why* CETUS is designed the way it is — the behavioral-science
mechanisms each feature targets — and frames the evidence **honestly**. Cue Exposure
Therapy (CET) is a plausible, theory-grounded adjunct, **not** a proven standalone
treatment: effects in rigorous trials are **small, mixed, and debated**.

> All primary claims below are supported by peer-reviewed literature retrieved from
> **PubMed**; DOIs are linked. CETUS is an adjunct under professional supervision, not a
> medical device. See [`DISCLAIMER.md`](DISCLAIMER.md).

---

## 1. Pavlovian conditioning & cue reactivity — *why we show cues*
Through repeated pairing with use, previously neutral stimuli (a glass, a lighter, a bar)
become **conditioned stimuli (CS)** that elicit **conditioned responses** — craving and
physiological arousal — even in abstinence. This is *cue reactivity*, the empirical
foundation of the whole approach.

- Carter & Tiffany, *Meta-analysis of cue-reactivity in addiction research*, **Addiction 1999** —
  across 41 studies, drug cues produced a large self-reported craving effect (ES ≈ **+0.92**;
  smaller for alcohol, ≈ +0.53) versus neutral cues. PubMed PMID 10605857.
- Norberg et al., cannabis cue-reactivity meta-analysis, **Addiction 2016** (moderate effect,
  d ≈ 0.59) — [10.1111/add.13472](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.13472).

**In CETUS:** graded, personalized cue playlists (images/video/sound) that escalate in
appetitive intensity; per-cue *reactivity* (peak − first rating) is reported so the clinician
sees which cues/contexts provoke the patient most.

## 2. Extinction learning & within-session habituation — *why we hold the cue, unreinforced*
Repeated, **unreinforced** exposure to the CS (cue present, no use) weakens the conditioned
craving response — Pavlovian **extinction**, observed within a session as **habituation** of
craving. Exposure is continued *until* craving subsides.

- Kiyak et al., CET-for-AUD **meta-analysis & systematic review, Addict Behav 2022** — CET rests
  on "well-established principles of Pavlovian extinction"; pooled effects were **small-to-medium**
  (drinks/day g ≈ −0.35; drinking-days g ≈ −0.30) with **GRADE quality rated low** —
  [10.1016/j.addbeh.2022.107578](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2022.107578).
- Powell, Gray & Bradley, opiate cue-exposure protocol, **Br J Clin Psychol 1993** — each exposure
  "continued until within-session habituation has occurred" —
  [10.1111/j.2044-8260.1993.tb01026.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8260.1993.tb01026.x).

**In CETUS:** the craving VAS (0–10) is sampled at baseline/peak/endpoint + periodically; the live
curve and the configurable *habituation criterion* (N consecutive low ratings) operationalize
"hold until habituated." The **within-session % reduction** (peak → endpoint) and the
**habituation slope** quantify it.

## 3. Spontaneous recovery & the context-dependence of extinction — *why we track across sessions*
Extinction does not erase the original learning; the conditioned response can **return between
sessions** (spontaneous recovery), especially in new contexts. Progress must therefore be tracked
*across* sessions, not just within one.

- Price et al., extinction of methamphetamine cue reactivity, **Behav Res Ther 2010** — explicitly
  examined between-session recovery of cue responding —
  [10.1016/j.brat.2010.05.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2010.05.010).

**In CETUS:** the report computes **between-session spontaneous recovery** (this session's baseline
minus the previous session's endpoint) and trends baseline/peak/endpoint and slope across sessions.

## 4. Affect as a craving modulator — *why stress matters and positive imagery helps*
Craving is state-dependent: **negative affect amplifies craving**, while **positive affect can
down-regulate it** (largely by reducing negative affect). Stress is a particularly strong relapse
context.

- Heckman et al., meta-analysis of affective manipulations on cigarette craving, **Addiction 2013** —
  negative-affect inductions produced a medium craving increase (g ≈ 0.47); positive-affect
  inductions reduced craving when they reduced negative affect —
  [10.1111/add.12284](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.12284).
- Schröder et al., VR-CET for smoking, **Sci Rep 2024** — **lower mean craving during exposure
  predicted 6-month abstinence**, and craving during the *stress* scenario best marked relapsers —
  [10.1038/s41598-024-58168-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-58168-7).

**In CETUS:** the **positive counter-stimuli gallery** (puppies/nature/the patient's own "reasons for
recovery") and the ability to **down-regulate the cue** (shrink/blur/dim/mute) give the patient
affect-regulation tools; **mean craving during exposure** is surfaced as an exploratory relapse-risk
signal.

## 5. Urge-specific coping skills (USCS) — *why exposure is paired with coping*
Modern CET does not extinguish craving in a vacuum; it pairs exposure with **active coping**, so the
patient rehearses skills in the presence of the cue. The four USCS steps are: name the feeling →
recall a negative consequence → recall a benefit of not using → choose an alternative action.

- Mellentin et al., CET-USCS smartphone app **design (2017)** and **RCT (2019, JMIR mHealth uHealth)** —
  [10.2196/13793](https://doi.org/10.2196/13793).
- Monti & Rohsenow, coping-skills + cue-exposure model, *Alcohol Res Health* 1999.

**In CETUS:** the guided **USCS panel** during exposure; coping events (and the patient's free-text)
are logged and shown per session.

## 6. Mechanistic monitoring & the "blocked habituation" caution
Because acute craving change does not reliably translate to outcomes, the **within-session
habituation slope** is treated as a mechanistic marker to watch — including the possibility that some
adjuncts *blunt* habituation.

- Ekhtiari et al., tDCS + cue reactivity in methamphetamine use disorder, **Hum Brain Mapp 2022** —
  raised the possibility that a single tDCS session can **block** normal within-session habituation —
  [10.1002/hbm.26007](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.26007).

**In CETUS:** the habituation slope is persisted per session and trended, so a clinician pairing CET
with neuromodulation can watch for a blocked-habituation pattern.

## Honest efficacy summary
- The single best head-to-head test of a purpose-built non-VR CET app (Mellentin 2019) was **null vs
  CBT** on drinking outcomes (non-inferior to in-person group CET).
- Pooled CET-for-AUD effects are **small-to-medium with low-quality evidence** (Kiyak 2022).
- CETUS's craving metrics are **descriptive and exploratory** — per-patient samples are small and no
  statistical inference is implied. Use them to inform clinical judgment, **not** as outcome measures.
